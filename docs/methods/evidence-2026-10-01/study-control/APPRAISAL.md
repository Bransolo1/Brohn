# Study controls, baselines and order: bounded appraisal

Reviewed 1 October 2026 against the immutable **combined-15** application. This is a four-family code and selected-literature appraisal, not a systematic review, protocol approval, scientific qualification or execution test. No R, browser, database, scientific job, application or registry change was made. Exact local inputs and the source manifest are in `BINDINGS.json`; source depths, claim links and independence are in `SOURCES.json` and `CLAIM-MATRIX.json`.

The existing starter is usable as an editable structure. Its numbers and role names do not establish an appropriate consumer experiment. The current constructor creates Control/Test conditions, two 5,000-ms stimuli, an after-each liking question, cyclic rotation, seed 104729, **no baseline phase (0 ms)** and a 500-ms fixation cue. The older paired contract instead defaults to no designated control and offers AB/BA plans. These contracts must not be conflated.

## Four consequential families

**Control and recorded baseline.** A condition role names the intended comparison; it neither creates a physiological reference recording nor establishes causality. The current UI already says this clearly. A nonzero `baseline_ms` schedules a blank phase before fixation; evidence of sensor recording, usable samples, context and an appropriate analysis window still has to come from the source and selected recipe. Mathôt et al. distinguish manipulation contrasts from within-trial pupil baselines and show baseline-artifact sensitivity using simulated and recorded data. Laborde et al.'s own recommendations distinguish baseline context from prior settling. Neither qualifies Brohn's blank display or a universal baseline transformation. [Mathôt et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-017-1007-2), [Laborde et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00213).

**Exposure, acclimatisation and recovery.** Exposure, fixation, settling and post-stimulus recovery answer different design needs. This stimulus loop has no separately typed acclimatisation or recovery step; a self-paced liking response is not automatically a controlled washout. In a study of 40 undergraduates hearing tones, Barry et al. found SCR differences with stimulus significance and presentation interval. That supports reviewing interval and repetition effects, not importing its auditory SOAs into packaging, adverts or UX. The HRV recommendation to settle before baseline is context-specific guidance, not independent empirical validation of a universal interval. Preserve all current timings; prospective protocols need purposes, acquisition context, observed intervals and an explicit rationale. [Barry et al. 2023, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10439882/), [Laborde et al. 2017, Baseline Recording](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00213).

**Order and carryover.** For two stimuli the compiler produces AB for one allocation index and BA for the next. It does **not** give each participant four trials ABBA. For three stimuli it rotates ABC/BCA/CAB: positions balance over a complete allocation cycle, but the only within-run transitions are AB, BC and CA. Reverse transitions are absent. This is a transparent mathematical example of the inspected expression, not an executed R test. Brooks's original supplement defines explicit predecessor balance; Greenwald explains why persistent or asymmetric effects can survive counterbalancing. Neither makes rotation a washout. Allocation counts include starts; dropout, exclusions and usable recording support can alter achieved balance. [Brooks 2012, supplement pp. 1–2](https://supp.apa.org/psycarticles/supplemental/a0029310/MET-Brooks20100184-R-S1.pdf), [Greenwald 1976, p. 318](https://faculty.washington.edu/agg/pdf/Gwald_PsychBull_1976.OCR.pdf).

**Particular concepts versus a stimulus population.** The paired recipe averages eligible condition observations within session, then paired sessions within person, then people. This protects against treating repeat visits as independent people; it does not estimate random stimulus variation. A question about these two particular packages is narrower than one about a class of packages or advertisements. Judd et al. and the independent Barr et al. analysis explain participant/item dependence; a new broader estimand needs a new design/model contract, not relabelling the current result. Westfall et al. remains an abstract-only power-planning lead here and shares Judd/Kenny authorship. Matuschek et al.'s abstract warns that maximal random-effects models can cost power; this appraisal therefore does not prescribe one universal mixed-model formula. [Judd et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028347), [Barr et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3881361/), [Matuschek et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2017.01.001).

## Action and limits

Keep the existing accurate role/baseline and rotation labels. `WORDING.md` proposes two small guidance clarifications and a future claim refinement; none changes the frozen registry. Before qualifying a new template, explicitly bind control rationale, intended generalisation, settling/baseline/recovery purposes, order assumptions, actual usable balance and a context-specific precision/power plan. Proposed fields in the matrix are requirements, not supported schema additions.

Seven publications have selected supporting text screened here; two further publications have metadata/abstract only. This includes original methods/simulations and primary publications of methodological recommendations, **not seven independent empirical consumer replications**. Independent teams corroborate narrower principles, while modality, consumer-task, device, population and exact-template transfer remain unresolved. No source supports treating 0/500/5,000 ms, two people, a fixed sample size or a statistical cutoff as universal best practice. All 50 capabilities and 17 work packages retain their separate existing statuses.
