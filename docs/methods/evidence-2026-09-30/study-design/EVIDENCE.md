# Study-design evidence audit — under review

This packet maps the inspected Brohn design/compiler paths to academic evidence and proposes the next design checks. It does not qualify a study template, change a calculation, or validate Brohn against the cited studies. The academic contract is `docs/methods/ACADEMIC-EVIDENCE-CONTRACT.md` at production commit `3aeb27f942f0359e0e7941df1c8a031dff4a28fb`. Exact inspected-file hashes are in [SOURCE.json](SOURCE.json).

Review date: 30 September 2026. Scope: consumer-study design, rather than every modality's analytical options. No production files, stores or saved results were changed; no R process, scientific job, replay or timing benchmark was run. All existing 50-capability/17-work-package statuses remain unchanged, including planned BWP16.

## What the application currently does

| Inspected seam | Code-evidenced behavior | Consequence for the next release |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `R/platform-core.R:58` `brohn_new_design` | `comparison`, `survey` and `blank` starters. Comparison creates A/control and B/test, two 5,000-ms stimuli; order `counterbalanced`, seed 104729, baseline 0 ms, fixation 500 ms; gaze and questionnaire selected. | These are editable presentation defaults. The inspected constructor does not bind them to a reviewed academic profile. Neither 5 seconds nor 500 ms is established here as universally adequate. |
| `R/platform-core.R:307` `brohn_compile` | Counterbalancing is cyclic rotation by allocation index; two conditions give AB/BA. Randomized order samples the stimulus sequence with a reproducible per-allocation seed. | Rotation over more than two conditions is not a general carryover-balanced design. Reproducibility does not guarantee balanced completed/usable observations or provide a between-participant allocation design. |
| `R/platform-delivery.R:16` and `:266` | Delivery persists allocation index and protocol JSON/hash; a trigger protects immutable protocol/index fields. Compilation retains realized stimulus order. | Retain this working provenance. Distinguish assigned order from observed exposure, completion, quality and achieved balance. The older CONTROL-DESIGN statement that delivery/allocation is not active is historical, not a description of current code. |
| `R/platform-core.R:350` and `R/platform-views.R:40` | Questions can occur before, after each stimulus or at the end. A baseline epoch is emitted only when baseline_ms > 0; fixation is a separate step. UI already calls order “Counterbalanced rotation” and says these are declared presentation settings. | A control condition, blank fixation, recorded physiological baseline, practice and recovery need distinct purposes. Baseline 0 means no epoch, not a measured zero signal or an adequate reference. Question option shuffling does not neutralize question-content priming. |
| `R/platform-analysis-plan.R:1` and `:12` | The registered paired plan covers gaze valid share, fixation dwell, numeric questionnaire response and scale scores. An AOI comparison checks that the label exists in both conditions. | Label equality does not establish comparable geometry, salience, visual angle, tracker uncertainty or target meaning. General between/mixed/stimulus-random-effects inference is not established by these inspected paths. |
| `R/platform-analysis-plan.R:56` | Paired results require identities; exposure values are averaged within session/condition, paired sessions within person, then people equally. Inference needs at least two people and handles zero observed variance without a misleading zero-width interval. | Preserve denominators and the person/session hierarchy; two people is an executable support condition, not a study-size recommendation. Sampling many timestamps or trials does not create independent participants. |
| `R/platform-analysis-plan.R:111` | Full declared families use Holm; incomplete modality families use conservative Bonferroni bounds over the full family. The output explicitly says the internal saved plan is not external preregistration or evidence of a confirmatory study. | Preserve both disclosures. This audit has not independently reproduced that procedure; original Holm supporting text still needs review. A citation to FDR research does not authorize replacing familywise error control. |
| `R/platform-library.R:85` | Templates retain source revision/hash and cloned lineage. | Evidence revision, review scope and option-specific literature are not part of this inspected template record. Add these prospectively through a versioned contract; never relabel old records. |

The master architecture describes broader within/between/mixed, hierarchy and prediction aspirations. It is not evidence that those paths are implemented. `docs/preparation/PROTOCOL-TEMPLATES.md` is a useful versioned recipe draft, but several timings and trial counts are vendor adaptations with single manual links. Its own cautions about practice, variants and individual reliability should remain visible; those pages need the option-level academic review separately.

## Evidence by consequential design choice

Each source ID links to a record in [REGISTRY.json](REGISTRY.json), including exact title, authors, DOI/stable URL, reviewed support, context and limits. The bibliography below provides direct primary-source links. “Reviewed” means the stated text was checked; it does not mean a Brohn procedure is accepted. Abstract-only checks support only the narrow abstract claim. S04 and S23 are metadata-only leads and are excluded from verified-support counts.

| Topic | Supporting sources and limits | Proposed Brohn requirement, still under review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Within/between/repeated exposure | S01 Charness et al.; S02 Greenwald distinguish efficiency from practice, sensitization and context effects. | Declare the estimand and whether first exposure, repeated use or learning is the target. Choose allocation and analysis together; identify irreversible carryover before suggesting a crossover. |
| Order and carryover | S03 Brooks addresses serial-order carryover; S01/S02 show why repeated treatments can change responses. S04 Williams remains a procedure-review lead. | Name cyclic rotation accurately. Offer an independently checked sequence scheme only for its specified carryover order, repetitions and constraints; report assigned and achieved balance. |
| Stimulus and AOI matching | S05 Orquin et al. and S06 Hessels et al. show that AOI decisions affect classification, with different geometry/noise contexts. | Freeze geometry, visual angle, display/scaling and AOI rationale with each stimulus. Matched names alone cannot pass a comparability check; sparse-face evidence does not set a universal margin for crowded packaging. |
| Luminance and pupil reference | S07 Mathôt explains physical determinants of pupil size; S08 Kret/Sjak-Shie and S09 Mathôt et al. address preprocessing and baseline artifacts. | Record luminance/background/viewing conditions and distinguish physical control from inferential attribution. Declare baseline support and correction recipe; never interpret every pupil change as emotion or effort. S07/S09 share an author and are not independent replications. |
| Acclimatisation, baseline and recovery | S10 Laborde et al. gives cardiac planning guidance. Independent EDA sources S11 Boucsein et al. and S12 Barry et al. establish modality-specific reporting and interval effects. | A physiological profile must specify actual recorded reference, posture/context, settling and response/recovery support for its metric. The auditory EDA study's intervals are study conditions, not defaults for advertisements. Cross-modality guidance is not independent validation of one exact cardiac recipe. |
| RT practice and timing | S02 covers practice-related design choice; S13 Deary et al. validates a named RT task in its study. S14 Anwyl-Irvine et al. and S15 Bridges et al. independently measure platform/configuration timing. | Bind practice, feedback, mapping, foreperiod, timeout and error rules to the exact task variant. Retain planned/observed times and test actual supported hardware. The timing papers evaluate their contemporary configurations, not Brohn. |
| Power, precision and hierarchy | S16 Lakens; S17 Westfall et al. justify designs at participant and stimulus levels. S18 Barr et al. and S19 Matuschek et al. differ on random-effects complexity/power tradeoffs. | Require an estimand, effect/precision rationale, useful-data assumptions and stopping rule; support crossed stimuli where generalisation requires them. Do not impose one N, automatically choose a maximal model, or treat every trial as a person. |
| Randomization, preregistration and exclusions | S20 Nosek et al.; S21 Simmons et al.; S22 CONSORT 2025 explanation. | Record allocation mechanism and concealment where relevant, planned exclusions and amendments, primary outcomes and external registration status. CONSORT is clinical reporting guidance transferred cautiously, not a consumer-study accreditation. |
| Multiplicity | S21 explains analytical flexibility; S24 Benjamini/Hochberg concerns a different error criterion. S23 Holm is a bibliographic lead pending full procedure review. | Declare family, direction, alpha and missing-family policy. Audit current Holm/Bonferroni implementation against original supporting text and an independent suitable comparison before marking that option reviewed-complete. FDR would be a separate explicit option and recipe. |
| Missingness and usable support | S25 Rubin defines assumptions for ignorability; S26 Little et al. emphasizes prevention, assumptions and sensitivity analysis. | Keep missing, excluded, unavailable and observed zero distinct. Expose participant/session/trial loss by condition and assess sensitivity rather than silently treating complete pairs as the full target sample. Clinical guidance requires adaptation to sensor/trial missingness. |
| Questionnaire placement and priming | S27 Strack et al. demonstrates question-context effects; S28 Morwitz et al. demonstrates that measuring intention can change later buying. | Prespecify placement, language and option assignment; explain when measurement is part of the intervention. Do not promise that merely moving every questionnaire to the end eliminates bias. |
| Reliability, validity and consumer prediction | S29 Hedge et al. separates robust mean effects from reliable individual differences; S30 Dang et al. distinguishes response processes. S31 Venkatraman et al. and S32 Falk et al. demonstrate prediction in specific neuroimaging/media settings. | Separate group contrast, person-level score, construct validity and out-of-sample consumer prediction. Require suitable external outcomes and validation for each claim; these papers do not validate a Brohn composite, facial emotion inference or individual diagnosis. BWP16 remains planned. |

## Next bounded work

1. Adopt an exact design-evidence record and prospectively bind it to template, compiled protocol, analysis and report revisions. Use [TEMPLATE-PREFLIGHT.md](TEMPLATE-PREFLIGHT.md) as the proposed seam.
2. Reconcile each actual selectable parameter with an option-level record. First review comparison exposure/order/baseline, AOI comparability and the paired-family procedure. Finish S04/S23 supporting-text access and independent procedure-level coverage before using them to confer a reviewed status.
3. Qualify the first named within-person comparison profile end to end with timing/support checks, achieved allocation, preserved exclusions and exact evidence export/history. A literature index alone cannot pass this gate.
4. Add between-participant and repeated-stimulus profiles only alongside their required allocation/inference paths. Keep method-specific physiology/RT records distinct; no universal timing or baseline preset.

This is a bounded initial audit, not a systematic review or complete option registry. The recommendations are deductions from the inspected code and the scoped literature, and require scientific review plus implementation/qualification. Bibliographic breadth cannot replace those steps.
