# Brohn introduction and launch plan

Introduce Brohn as a premium research studio that brings implicit tasks, explicit responses and biovital evidence into one guided workflow, so a first-time operator can finish a study and a commercial team can repeat it with confidence.

Prepared 2026-09-08 using the innovation-first-product-psychology skill. This is a product and launch plan; targets, copy and storyboards below are proposals. No outreach, publication or participant testing is represented as completed. The [master architecture](../MASTER-ARCHITECTURE.md) and [50-capability register](../preparation/capability-register.json) govern scope and availability.

## Positioning and psychological promise

**Category:** an R-powered, open research studio. **Value:** controlled study design, linked measurement and automated analysis in a comprehensible sequence. **Promise:** “I can prepare, run, recover and explain this study.”

The commercial audience wants a repeatable way to compare creative, packaging, interfaces or concepts without hand-joining exports. The undergraduate operator needs understandable choices, visible progress and an explanation they can use in a methods section. These are complementary needs: the novice-facing flow becomes a reliable operation a commercial team can standardize.

Lead with the research outcome, then reveal the breadth. The first demonstration compares two designs with eye tracking and liking; the same workspace supports the declared EEG, EDA, cardiac, respiration, facial, audio, movement and implicit-method families as their profiles become available. A guided eye-study entry point must never imply that Brohn is only an eye-tracking utility.

Three differentiators should be visible in the product:

- **One study, linked evidence:** every measurement and answer refers to the right participant, stimulus and phase.
- **Automation that can be inspected:** quality rules, AOI proposals, analysis and reports run from a pinned recipe, with clear correction paths.
- **A study you can reuse:** versioned protocols, source data, decisions and methods travel together.

Competitors can copy a builder screen quickly. Reproducible recipes, adapter fixtures, accessible tasks and replayable reports are harder to reproduce. Community contributions should strengthen those assets using consented or synthetic examples.

## External introduction and landing copy

Use the soft dark foundation, mint/sage emphasis and lavender secondary accents consistently with the Brohn brand assets. Favor generous space, legible type, precise labels and one primary action. The tone is calm and capable. Avoid “mind reading,” “objective emotion,” “effortless science” or unsupported speed comparisons.

**Preview-stage copy, usable before public release**

> **Brohn**\
> **Bring the whole study together.**\
> An R-powered research studio in development for implicit tasks, questionnaires and biovital data. We are building a guided route from study design to reproducible results.\
> **Explore the working preview** · Read the build plan

**Public-beta copy, activated when the integrated journey exists**

> **From study idea to evidence, in one place.**\
> Design controlled studies, connect responses and signals, and turn supported recordings into clear, reproducible reports. Start with a guided eye-tracking comparison and extend your study with the methods you need.\
> **Try a sample study** · View the source

Below the hero, show the five-stage journey: **Plan → Questions → Collect → Review → Results**. Then show an actual report, a short explanation of controls, the supported-method catalogue and a compact “what works in this release” panel generated from release evidence. Put breadth beside readiness; avoid treating all 50 planning entries as available features.

Use one concrete story: “Did the new pack draw attention to the ingredient claim, and did people like it?” Show gaze allocation and reported liking separately, with a control comparison and usable data counts. A final section explains that advanced settings and R-compatible exports remain available when needed.

## Ninety-second demo storyboard

| Time | Screen and action | Narration purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | Show two packaging designs and the research question; open a finished fictional sample report. | Establish the outcome immediately: notice, liking and their comparison. Clearly label sample data. |
| 10–22 s | Choose the comparison recipe; add the two images and designate control/test. | Demonstrate a guided start with explicit design intent. |
| 22–32 s | Show the planned AB/BA order and viewing/rating/rest phases; open participant preview. | Explain that the workflow includes experimental control and execution. |
| 32–43 s | Add a liking question tied to each exposure; briefly reveal the questionnaire palette and flow. | Make explicit responses a central part of the study. |
| 43–55 s | Select the supported import or collection route; show preparation and session progress. | Show one actual route available in the demonstrated release. |
| 55–67 s | Review an AOI proposal and one seeded recoverable quality issue; make a correction. | Show automation plus understanding and recovery. |
| 67–78 s | Results update with uncertainty, usable support and linked liking. Reveal other available modality cards. | Show the complete result and the platform's extensibility without mixing signals into a generic score. |
| 78–90 s | Export the reproducible report, duplicate the study, and end on “Try a sample study.” | Close with portability, reuse and a clear next action. |

Record the implemented release; label any concept frames explicitly. Provide captions, transcript, reduced-motion alternatives and readable cursor/focus cues. A technical companion can explain the method manifest and R architecture.

## First-run journey: sample to independent study

**1. Arrive at a result.** Offer “Explore a sample” as the primary entry and “Start my study” alongside it. Avoid an account form or hardware selection before the user understands the value. The sample explains one comparison, highlights its controls and identifies fictional observations.

**2. Make one meaningful change.** Ask the user to inspect or adjust an AOI and see which result changes. Preserve the original sample. Feedback explains the dependency rather than celebrating an arbitrary click: “The report now uses your revised area.”

**3. Transfer to the user's question.** “Use this study structure” creates an independent draft. A short question-led flow asks for stimuli, comparison intent and collection route. Suggested settings are named and inspectable. Explain why a control or baseline is requested beside the relevant choice.

**4. Preview and prepare.** Show the actual participant sequence, response mapping and linked questions. One preparation panel gathers missing assets, calibration, consent and storage issues, each with a direct fix. Save progress automatically and allow a safe exit.

**5. Reach the first report.** Finalization starts the eligible jobs. Progress uses understandable stages such as “Checking recordings” and “Calculating the comparison.” The review queue contains actionable exceptions; complete data proceeds automatically.

**6. Earn independence.** Finish with the report and three answerable prompts: what was compared, which data contributed, and what the result supports. Offer export, methods details and duplicate-study actions. Completion is a calm milestone, not confetti or an inflated researcher badge.

Teaching adds a tutor walkthrough and worked example; teams add reusable templates and review handoff. Both use the same study, data and method contracts.

## Product psychology translated into build requirements

| Observation and mechanism | Build response | Smallest useful proof |
|---|---|---|
| A large modality catalogue can make novices feel unqualified before starting. | Lead with research questions and a guided sample; reveal methods through meaningful additions. | Compare time and unaided completion against a catalogue-first entry. |
| Automatic reports can increase confidence faster than comprehension. | Keep measured outcome, interpretation and usable support visible; offer a short explanation check. | Compare confidence with accuracy on the three first-report prompts. |
| A calibration or import problem can turn one failure into abandonment. | Explain the issue in context, preserve progress and give one recovery action. | Seed one recoverable error; measure successful recovery and assistance. |
| Low click counts can hide difficult choices or keyboard barriers. | Measure decisions, actions and effort separately; provide equivalent keyboard paths. | Observe complete tasks with novice and accessibility participants. |
| Teams may trust a polished result but struggle to reproduce it. | Export inputs, recipe, decisions and report together; show the release evidence. | Ask another researcher to replay and explain the same result. |

## Staged introduction

**Design-partner preparation:** package the working preview, sample, supported-route inventory and focused walkthrough. Recruit a proposed initial group of 3–5 commercial/UX teams and 2 teaching settings when outreach is authorized. Seek variety in methods, operator experience and access needs. These numbers are planning targets, not existing partners.

**Private beta:** deliver the complete guided eye-study route and questionnaire linkage, then integrate broader modality packs through the master build packages. Run task observation and recovery sessions. Capture requested methods against capability IDs so feedback improves the full platform instead of generating disconnected custom screens.

**Public open-source beta:** publish once repository destination, licence, installation, example-data rights, contribution process and release evidence are concrete. Lead with what a newcomer can complete that day. Provide a supported-method matrix, reproducible sample and clear extension destinations.

**Stable introduction:** strengthen reliability, recovery, accessibility and supported adapter coverage from beta evidence. Release method packs with their own change notes and reproducible examples. Graduate named live-device routes and stronger scientific claims when the relevant evidence exists.

Pair each release with a useful research story and demonstrated capability improvement. This plan creates no outreach or announcements.

## Proof, targets and assets

**Claim now:** working local foundations and documented preparation, with the exact evidence described in project status. **After integrated prototype:** supported import/run-to-report workflows demonstrated on the release. **After observed use:** measured completion, time, comprehension and accessibility findings. **After method/device evidence:** claims scoped to the tested procedure, data and configuration.

Initial beta targets, to revise after the first observed baseline:

| Product metric | Capability counterpart | Proposed target |
|---|---|---|
| Sample activation | Correctly explains comparison and data support | ≥80% unaided completion; sample journey ≤5 active minutes |
| Own-study creation | Correct control, question linkage and planned order | ≤15 active setup minutes for the supported two-image recipe, excluding acquisition |
| Recovery completion | Understands the corrected issue | ≥80% recover one seeded issue without facilitator intervention |
| Study reuse | Repeats the workflow independently | Track a second completed study within 30 days alongside assistance needed |
| Team adoption | Another researcher reproduces the result | Track replay success, discrepancies and comprehension together |

Report denominators, assistance and failed attempts. These are directional formative targets, not achieved statistics or universal promises. Track clicks/keystrokes, meaningful decisions and perceived effort without recording participants' research content in product analytics by default.

Launch assets checklist:

- Stage-appropriate landing copy, native logo/icon files and accessible brand tokens.
- Actual sample study, linked questionnaire, reproducible report and methods appendix.
- Captioned 90-second demonstration, transcript and supported-route walkthrough.
- Installation guide, capability/release matrix and troubleshooting/recovery guide.
- Repository README, licence, contribution template and reference-data guidance.
- Design-partner session script, comprehension prompts and metric definitions.
- Draft announcement, teaching handout and commercial case-study template.
- Final release evidence review: every demonstrated screen and claim matches the shipped version.
